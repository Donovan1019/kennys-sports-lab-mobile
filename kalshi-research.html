Kalshi Learning Lab · Start Here

Educational examples, not trade recommendations. Prices shown on the live board may change or be stale.

1. What does YES at 60¢ mean?

One YES contract costs 60¢ before fees. If the stated event settles YES, it pays $1; if NO, it pays $0. Your maximum contract loss is the purchase price plus applicable fees. A 60¢ ask is an executable price, not a verified 60% chance.

2. YES versus NO · how do I compare?

Compare the YES ask to your independently estimated YES probability. Compare the NO ask to 100% minus that estimate. Evaluate each side separately after the applicable fee and verify the exact settlement wording. Do not assume YES ask plus NO ask equals $1.

3. Bid, ask, spread and liquidity

Ask is the quoted price to buy. Bid is the quoted price to sell. The difference is the spread. A narrow spread can reduce execution friction but does not create an edge. Positive historical volume does not guarantee enough size is available at the displayed price.

4. Expected value and break-even

For a $1 payout, expected net profit per YES contract in cents is 100 × estimated YES probability − entry price in cents − total expected fees in cents. Break-even probability is (price + fees) / 100. This is a simplified hold-to-settlement calculation; use the contract-specific fee schedule and consider settlement uncertainty.

5. Why a 99¢ favorite may be a bad trade

A 99¢ YES ask offers at most 1¢ gross profit and almost the entire 99¢ can be lost. Fees may eliminate the upside. A low price, such as 1¢, is not automatically a bargain either.

6. Kenny's Diamond standard

GREEN DIAMOND requires a fresh, uniquely matched, calibrated independent probability, validated settlement terms, executable prices, applicable fees, sufficient liquidity and a meaningful positive edge. Research-only projections stay UNVERIFIED. No picks are issued when these checks are missing.
